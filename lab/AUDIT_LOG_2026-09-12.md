# v6 dossier — independent verification & audit (2026-09-12)

The attached "near-scalper-bot-v6-dossier.pdf" arrived as an uploaded file claiming
pre-run backtest results (chop-gate + wall-TP strategy vs baseline). It was not
produced by me in this conversation and its claims were not taken on faith — every
number was independently reproduced by transcribing its own appendix code verbatim
and running it against the real data_<PAIR>_30d.json files in this repo.

## 1. Reproduction — PASS
backtest_v6.py + validate_v6.py, run unmodified: baseline 1,666 trades/-143.4%/-0.086%,
v6 740 trades/-51.5%/-0.070%, walk-forward folds, bootstrap CIs, Kelly buckets, and the
Monte Carlo survivability numbers (median $9.47, P(ruin)=0%, DD 6.7%/8.6%/11.3%) all
reproduce to 3+ decimal places. The code is real and executes as described.

## 2. Methodology defect found — the PDF's own "baseline" is mislabeled
backtest_v6.py's `simulate_pair()` computes `dynamic_tp_distance()` (the wall-TP
logic) UNCONDITIONALLY, regardless of the `gate_chop` flag. This means the row
labeled "Baseline (v5 logic, no chop gate)" in the PDF's executive summary already
includes the wall-based TP change — it is not a clean v5 reproduction. Confirmed by
ablation: a truly clean baseline (fixed 1.6xATR TP, no gate) scores n=1705/-144.47%,
close to but distinct from the reported "baseline" (n=1666/-143.4%, which is actually
"wall-TP-only, no gate").

## 3. Ablation — isolates the true source of v6's improvement
2x2 test (chop-gate x wall-TP), independent code, same data:
  A baseline (no gate, fixed TP):     -0.0847%/trade
  B chop-gate ONLY (fixed TP):        -0.0698%/trade  (delta +0.0149)
  C wall-TP ONLY (no gate):           -0.0861%/trade  (delta -0.0013, i.e. slightly worse)
  D both (the reported "v6"):         -0.0696%/trade  (delta +0.0151)
Conclusion: 100% of v6's improvement comes from the chop gate. The wall-TP component
contributes ~nothing on its own (mildly negative) — consistent with, and explaining,
the PDF's own section 4.4 finding that "wall-widened TP underperformed."

## 4. Mechanical artifact in the chop-gate result
V6's TREND-regime trade count (724) does not equal baseline's TREND-regime trade
count (482) under the single-position-at-a-time methodology. Skipping a chop entry
frees the one available slot sooner, letting additional trend entries fire that would
have been blocked by a concurrent chop position in baseline. Part of the chop gate's
measured benefit is this slot-freeing effect, not purely "avoided a bad trade." This
methodology (deliberately single-slot, disclosed in the PDF) may overstate the
improvement relative to live's actual 8-slot concurrency, where slots are less
often the binding constraint. Flagged as a needed follow-up test (multi-slot ablation).

## 5. Fee/slippage sensitivity — the central finding
Chop-gate-only variant, gross (zero-cost) vs live costs:
  zero cost:            +0.0840%/trade  (a real, if thin, gross edge exists)
  live fees (0.06/0.01%): -0.0698%/trade
  2x fees:               -0.1582%/trade
  3x slippage:           -0.1002%/trade
  stress both:           -0.1954%/trade
Walk-forward on the gross (zero-cost) edge: fold1 +0.147%, fold2 +0.048%, fold3
+0.056%/trade. Live round-trip cost is ~0.14%/trade (2x0.06% fee + 2x0.01% slip).
Only fold1's gross edge clears that bar; folds 2-3 do not.

## Verdict this feeds into the REFINE/REPLACE/HYBRID call
The chop-gated EMA-pullback signal has a real, walk-forward-positive GROSS edge —
it is not random. But the edge is thinner than round-trip transaction costs in 2 of
3 folds, meaning this system is currently FEE-LIMITED, not signal-limited. Adding
more signal complexity (liquidity sweeps, MSS/BOS, FVG, etc.) will not fix a problem
whose root cause is "the edge per trade is smaller than the cost per trade." The
higher-leverage fixes are: (a) larger targets to amortize the fixed per-trade fee
(fewer, bigger trades), or (b) lower round-trip cost (maker fills, a cheaper venue),
before any new signal features are worth testing.

## 6. Multi-slot concurrency ablation (owner-authorized, same day) — THE DECISIVE TEST
Question: does the chop gate's benefit survive REAL 8-slot concurrency (live fund
handling: 8 global slots, NEAR 3-cap, 90s cooldown, 12% daily loss limit wired,
25% DD wall with 1/4 recovery sizing, equity compounding, entry-locked sizing)?
Method: multislot_ablation.py — both arms share identical machinery; the ONLY
difference is the chop gate. Timestamp-keyed timeline (pairs not perfectly
bar-aligned — SOL/XRP start 60s later; verified before running). Level-fill
conventions identical to the audited single-slot lab. Bootstrap on the
two-arm mean difference, 10k resamples, seed 13.

Results:
  ARM A (no gate):   n=1219  WR 91.2%  -0.0417%/trade  final $6.52  maxDD 40.7%  daily trips 2
  ARM B (chop gate): n=1183  WR 90.7%  -0.0533%/trade  final $6.54  maxDD 43.9%  daily trips 1
  Difference A-B: +0.0108%/trade, 95% CI [-0.071%, +0.094%] — NOT statistically
  distinguishable from zero. The chop gate provides no measurable benefit under
  real concurrency.

Decisive secondary finding: in the ungated arm, CHOP entries (-0.0391%/trade,
n=784) score INDISTINGUISHABLY from TREND entries (-0.0453%/trade, n=404).
The 15m efficiency-ratio regime classifier (ER>=0.38) carries no discriminative
power over trade outcomes in this window. The single-slot "chop gate" benefit
(section 3) was entirely a slot-freeing artifact.

Conclusions feeding the REFINE/REPLACE/HYBRID decision:
  1. The chop gate (as defined by this classifier) is NOT the fix. Burden of proof
     now sits with ANY new gate: it must show benefit in the multi-slot lab.
  2. The fee-limited diagnosis is reinforced: gross edge +0.084%/trade vs
     ~0.14% round-trip cost; both arms negative net under full live machinery.
  3. The risk stack works mechanically (daily limit trips fire, recovery sizing
     engages) but cannot rescue negative expectancy — both arms end ~$6.5 from
     $10 with ~40% compounded maxDD over the 30d window.
  4. Structural levers remain the priority: larger targets (amortize the fixed
     fee) or cheaper execution — not more signal complexity.

Limitations of this study (disclosed): minute-level entry evaluation vs live's
~4-min tick cadence; the live EV-after-costs gate not modeled; level fills with
MAE>BE>TP within-bar ordering; fixed pair evaluation order; single 30-day window
(albeit walk-forwarded); the regime definition tested is the dossier's ER
implementation — a different chop definition is NOT refuted by this test.

## 7. TP-size sweep under multi-slot live machinery (owner-authorized, same day)
Question: does scaling TP up (k x ATR, k=1.6..8) amortize the fixed per-trade fee
and turn the strategy net-positive? Answer: NO — bigger targets are monotonically
WORSE beyond k=2, and no k is net-positive at live costs.

  k |     n |    WR | gross/trade |  NET/trade |  95% CI        | final$  maxDD
 1.6 | 1219 | 91.2% |   +0.0983%  |  -0.0417%  | [-0.098,+0.014] | 6.519  40.7%
 2.0 | 1201 | 91.3% |   +0.1007%  |  -0.0393%  | [-0.096,+0.017] | 7.000  36.4%
 3.0 | 1047 | 89.6% |   +0.0669%  |  -0.0731%  | [-0.142,-0.008] | 7.142  41.3%
 4.0 |  984 | 88.8% |   +0.0625%  |  -0.0775%  | [-0.153,-0.005] | 7.066  45.3%
 5.0 |  852 | 86.2% |   -0.0193%  |  -0.1593%  | [-0.249,-0.072] | 5.756  45.9%
 6.0 |  767 | 84.6% |   -0.0413%  |  -0.1813%  | [-0.283,-0.082] | 5.890  45.6%

Why: the signal's favorable-excursion distribution is bounded — raising TP does not
capture more move, it converts TPs into BE_STOPs/MAX_AGEs while MAE walls stay

gross at k>=5. Walk-forward at k=2: fold1 +0.23%, fold2 -4.6%, fold3 -7.5%
(net_frac sums) — the edge does not persist across the window.

## 8. Execution-cost scenarios (the other fee-limited lever)
k=1.6 / k=2.0 under: live taker (0.06%+0.01%), maker (0.02%+0.01%), maker+zero-slip.
  k=2 maker+zero-slip is the ONLY net-positive cell: +0.0063%/trade, CI [-0.059,+0.070]
  — still statistically indistinguishable from zero.
  Even near-perfect execution does not make this signal pay in this window.

## Verdict contributed to the REFINE/REPLACE/HYBRID decision
On this 30-day window, with full live fund-handling modeled, the EMA-pullback scalp
is structurally fee-limited: gross edge +0.08-0.10%/trade < any realistic round-trip
cost. Neither the chop gate (no benefit under concurrency), nor wall-based TPs
(no isolated benefit), nor bigger targets (monotonically worse), nor cheaper
execution (still indistinguishable from zero) rescues it. Recommendation to owner:
do NOT lock TP changes (k=2 vs 1.6 indistinguishable). The strategy as-is is safe
to run as a paper learning rig with the current risk stack; making it profitable
requires a signal with larger favorable excursions per trade (REPLACE territory)
or a substantially different cost/holding structure. Caveats: single 30d window,
paper-level level-fills, walls/ages fixed at registry values, no EV gate modeled.

## 9. Laddered-TP exit proposal (owner idea, voice 2026-09-12) — tested under live machinery
Proposal: instead of one TP + one BE, a ladder of 4 levels (25/50/75/100% of TP
distance); profits banked at each level; stop ratchets to the highest passed level
so a retrace exits there instead of at breakeven. Two variants isolated, both
against the current BASE exit (BE armed at 50% of TP distance):
  R (ratchet-only): stop trails the last passed level, single exit, no partials.
  P (owner's full idea): bank 25% of notional at each of levels 1-3 + 25% runner.

Results (8-slot machinery, no regime gate, level fills, 10k bootstrap):
  k=1.6: BASE -0.0417%/t [CI -0.098,+0.014]  R -0.0214% [CI -0.055,+0.011]  P -0.0617% [CI -0.093,-0.031]
  k=2.0: BASE -0.0393%/t [CI -0.096,+0.017]  R -0.0269% [CI -0.061,+0.006]  P -0.0659% [CI -0.099,-0.035]
  Diff vs BASE: R +0.020%/t (k1.6) CI [-0.046,+0.088] — NOT significant.
                P -0.020%/t (k1.6) CI [-0.084,+0.045] — NOT significant (harm not proven either, but direction consistent).

Mechanics (why): R converts BASE's 611 BE_STOPs (+0.05% each) into 1,646 RATCHET
exits (+0.09% each) and cuts full TPs from 485 to 243 — WR 91% -> 95%, maxDD
40.7% -> 33.0%, final $6.52 -> $6.99 at k=1.6. Trade count rises 1,219 -> 1,993
(ratchet exits free slots sooner — same slot-freeing class of effect flagged in §6).
P's banking halves the size of the TP cohort's winners (TP mean +0.49% -> +0.25%)
without lifting the ratchet cohort enough to pay for it: selling the only paying
cohort early is a drag, as predicted.

Verdict vs the falsification bar: NEITHER variant passes B3 (net CI entirely
above zero) on this window. No registry lock. R is the better half and worth
re-testing on fresh windows (mechanism sound, effect size within noise here);
P's banking is not recommended — directionally harmful at both k values.

## 10. The BE problem — anatomy, ceiling, and the fix (owner question, 2026-09-12)
"Continuously hitting BE" decomposed with per-trade MFE telemetry (base exit, k=1.6, n=1219):

ANATOMY: the BE cohort is 49.8% of all trades (607). Their mean MFE was +0.429%
(median +0.403%, p90 +0.562%) — the wiggle is REAL, then price retrace gave it
all back to entry. They captured +0.05% of the +260% (account-%) that sat at
their peaks — an 11.7% capture rate. Root cause: trade scale equals noise scale
(TP = 1.6xATR ~ 0.5-0.8%; the ATR-sized noise that reaches half the target is the
same noise that retraces it). BE frequency is a symptom of scale, not a stop
placement error.

CEILING (hindsight bound): perfect sell-every-peak exits = +0.4003%/trade net vs
actual -0.0417% — a +0.44%/trade gap. But peaks are only knowable in hindsight;
the realistic ratchet closed ~5% of that gap (0.0203/0.442). Exit engineering
alone cannot rescue this strategy — the gap is mostly noise, not inefficiency.

FIX, measured in layers:
  L1 exit patch — ratchet (already §9): -0.042 -> -0.021%/trade, maxDD 40.7 -> 33.0%.
  L2 cost lever — ratchet + maker execution (0.02% fee):
       maker 0.01% slip: +0.0678%/trade, CI [+0.035%, +0.100%] — ENTIRELY ABOVE ZERO.
        folds +0.056/+0.123/+0.024 (all positive, B4 pass), final $41.60, maxDD 22.4%.
        FIRST configuration to pass B3+B4 on this window.
       3x slip stress: +0.0278%, CI [-0.005%, +0.060%] — crosses zero, fold3 negative.
        FAILS the slip-stress trap; fragile to execution assumptions.
       zero slip: +0.0887%, CI [+0.056%, +0.121%], folds all positive.
  L3 scale (structural): trade at a scale where move >> noise and >> cost — the
       replacement signal per B1. Robust to execution assumptions.

Caveats on L2 (material): maker fills = limit orders = fill uncertainty and
adverse selection (fills skew toward failing signals) — unmodelable in this
backtest; requires live shadow (Stage 3) before any lock. Single 30d window.
The paper engine currently simulates taker fills only; maker entries are an
engine change (order-type), not a parameter change.

## 11. Split-TP + raised-BE proposal (owner idea #3) — tested, FAILS vs pure ratchet
Proposal: bank 50% of notional at 75% of TP distance, runner to full TP; BE stop
raised to entry + 25% of TP distance (armed at 50% MFE). k=1.6, 8-slot machinery.

  TAKER: net -0.0630%/trade, CI [-0.119%, -0.010%] — ENTIRELY BELOW ZERO
         (confidently worse than doing nothing). Final $5.83, maxDD 48.4% (worst measured).
  MAKER: net +0.0170%/trade, CI [-0.039%, +0.070%] — crosses zero.

vs the PURE RATCHET from §9/§10: taker -0.0214 (dominates -0.0630),
maker +0.0678 CI[+0.035,+0.100] (dominates +0.0170), maxDD 33.0% (dominates 48.4%).

Why the combo loses to the ratchet on every metric:
  1. Banking shaves the only paying cohort (TP mean +0.477% -> +0.393%).
  2. A FIXED raised BE stops trades prematurely; the ratchet raises the stop
     PROGRESSIVELY (pass 50% -> stop at 25%, pass 75% -> stop at 50%), so it
     keeps more of each move than a static "BE + a little bit".
  3. The fixed 25% stop lets banked runners give back from 75% all the way to 25%
     before exiting — the ratchet would have stopped them at 50%.
Owner's raised-BE instinct is directionally right — the ratchet IS that idea done
progressively. The TP-split/banking half is the one to drop (consistent with §9's
P-variant finding: selling winners early is a drag).
